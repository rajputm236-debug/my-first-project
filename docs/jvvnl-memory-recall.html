What this is. A sourced, memory-based compilation of questions that candidates and teachers recalled
after the JVVNL / RVUNL Junior Accountant computer-based exam (18 and 21 September 2021). The exam is
a CBT, so no official question paper exists. Every item below is someone's recollection. It is not an official
paper, and it does not contain an answer key.

How to use this document
Read in this order: the topic summary, then each topic section, then the conflict list and the 2018 appendix.
The list of sources is at the very end, so the question pages stay clean.

Ground rules followed
• Memory-based only. Video recall and comment recall are labelled and kept apart. Student comments are
not official evidence.
• No answer key. No answers are copied from any compilation, teacher or comment. Where a comment
contained an answer, only the question was kept.
• Different recalled versions of one question are shown side by side, not merged. See the conflict list.
• Questions are translated into plain exam-style English. The wording is not the official wording.
• Tax and law questions are 2021 recalls. Check rates, limits and dates against current rules before using
them to study.
• 2018 material is kept in a separate appendix and is not counted in the 2021 numbers.

Reported exam structure (unverified)
The supplied compilation reports the 2021 paper as Part A (accounts and commerce, 60 questions of 2
marks each) and Part B (Rajasthan GK, India GK, maths, reasoning, English, Hindi; 80 questions of 1 mark
each), 200 marks in total, in two shifts on 18 September and one on 21 September. Viewer comments say
Part A had many numericals and that GK went deep into Rajasthan static and current facts. These are not
official figures and could not be checked against a notification.

What was searched
Public YouTube review videos about the 2021 exam week and their comment sections. This version widened
the search beyond the first compilation to more channels, more videos and more comment threads. About
40 comment sources and 6 video sources support the entries. See the last section for the full list and the
limits of the search.

Patterns reported by viewers
• Viewers repeatedly said Part A leaned on costing, GST, income tax, auditing and accounting standards, and
that corporate accounting was light. Several commenters said GST questions were advanced rather than
basic. This is opinion, not a count from an official paper.
• Viewers described the Rajasthan GK in Part B as deep and fact-heavy (districts, forts, lakes, unification
phases, 2021 current affairs). Several commenters said they could attempt only a few of those questions.
